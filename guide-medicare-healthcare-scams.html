Medicare Card, Genetic Testing & Medical Billing Scams: Protecting Seniors from Spoofed Calls

By Hemendra K., IAM Specialist & Zero-Trust Architect • Peer-Reviewed by Dr. Priyanka Ameta, AI Research & Academics • Updated: November 2026 • 11 min read

According to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General (HHS-OIG), medical identity theft and fraudulent Medicare billing cost public healthcare programs tens of billions of dollars annually while placing individual beneficiaries at severe personal risk. Unlike credit card fraud—where a stolen number can be canceled in seconds—a stolen Medicare Beneficiary Identifier (MBI) allows illicit billing syndicates to submit thousands of dollars in phantom claims for Durable Medical Equipment (DME), high-cost genetic cancer panels, and telemedicine consultations under the victim's medical chart.

Even more dangerously, when fraudulent diagnoses or prescription histories are billed against a senior's Medicare ID, they can corrupt electronic health records or exhaust annual coverage limits—preventing the victim from receiving legitimate wheel-chairs, braces, or diagnostic tests when prescribed by their real physician.

🔬 The Three Primary Lures: 'Plastic Chip Cards,' 'Free DNA Kits,' and 'Refund Direct Deposits'

Healthcare fraud call centers peak during the annual Medicare Open Enrollment period (October 15 through December 7), using automated VoIP dialers that spoof local area codes or the official 1-800-MEDICARE number. Their primary objective is simple: trick the beneficiary into reading aloud the 11-character alphanumeric Medicare Beneficiary Identifier (MBI) printed on their red-white-and-blue card, or confirming their Social Security number and bank routing details.

Once the caller harvests the MBI, a corrupt telemedicine broker pays a contracted physician a small fee to rubber-stamp an order for unnecessary orthotic back braces, knee braces, continuous glucose monitors, or cheek-swab genetic testing kits. The syndicate bills Medicare $1,500 to $12,000 per beneficiary, ships a cheap $15 brace to the senior's house to create a postal tracking receipt, and vanishes before CMS auditors flag the provider number.

Annotated Script Breakdown: The 'New Plastic Chip Medicare Card' Call

Scammer: "Hello, I'm calling from the Medicare Benefits Processing Desk. Beginning next month, all paper Medicare cards are being deactivated and replaced with new plastic microchip security cards. To avoid a lapse in your Part B doctor coverage, please read me the 11-digit number on your current card so we can mail your chip card today."

Forensic Reality: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) exclusively issues paper Medicare cards. There is no plastic card, no microchip card, and no black-and-gold card. Furthermore, if Medicare actually already had you on the phone as an official agency, they would already have your MBI on file and would never cold-call you to ask for it.

🚩 High-Confidence Forensic Red Flags

During incident investigations, ScamShield Lab researchers consistently identify the following behavioral and technical indicators across confirmed attacks:

🚨 Claims That Paper Medicare Cards Are Expiring or Being Replaced With 'Microchip Cards'

Medicare cards do not expire and do not have microchips or PIN numbers. Any caller claiming you must 'upgrade' your card to keep your benefits is an identity thief.

🚨 Offers of 'Free' Back Braces, Pain Creams, or DNA Cancer Cheek-Swab Kits

Whether at a senior health fair, door-to-door, or over the phone, never accept a 'free' genetic cheek swab or orthopedic brace from anyone other than your own treating physician. Scammers use a single swab to bill Medicare up to $10,000.

🚨 Unsolicited Medical Lab Portal SMS Links ('View Your Urgent Lab Results or Unpaid Co-Pay')

Phishing syndicates send SMS messages mimicking Quest Diagnostics, Labcorp, or local hospital networks claiming a $14.50 co-pay is overdue before your test results can be released—leading to a credit-card harvesting page.

🚨 Offers of a 'Medicare Part B Cash Rebate' Direct-Deposited Into Your Checking Account

Callers claim you qualify for a $148/month Social Security give-back rebate and ask for your bank routing and checking account numbers to 'set up direct deposit'—then initiate unauthorized ACH debit withdrawals.

🛡️ Step-by-Step Containment & Prevention Protocol

If you or a household member encounters this threat vector, execute the following containment sequence immediately:

Step 1: Treat Your Medicare Card Like a Credit Card & Audit Your Quarterly MSN

Never read your Medicare number to an unexpected caller. Every three months, review your Medicare Summary Notice (MSN) or Medicare Advantage Explanation of Benefits (EOB) line by line. Look for unfamiliar doctors, out-of-state durable medical equipment companies, or genetic laboratory tests you never received.

Step 2: Refuse & Return Unordered Medical Braces or Swab Kits

If a box of back braces, catheter supplies, or diabetic strips arrives at your home that your doctor did not prescribe, do not sign for it—mark the package 'Refused — Return to Sender' and photograph the shipping label.

Step 3: Report Suspected Compromises to 1-800-MEDICARE & HHS-OIG

If you accidentally shared your Medicare number with a caller, call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) immediately to flag your account and request a replacement MBI, and report the fraud to the HHS Office of Inspector General at 1-800-HHS-TIPS (1-800-447-8477) or your state Senior Medicare Patrol (smpresource.org).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can Medicare issue me a brand-new Medicare Beneficiary Identifier (MBI) if mine was stolen?

Yes. If your Medicare number has been compromised or you spot unauthorized claims on your Medicare Summary Notice, call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) and explain that your number was exposed to a scammer. CMS will deactivate the compromised MBI and mail a replacement paper card with a new 11-character identifier.

Are all TV commercials and phone calls about 'Medicare Advantage flex cards' scams?

While some private Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental allowance cards for groceries or over-the-counter items in specific zip codes, lead-generation call centers frequently use misleading 'Free $2,500 Government Flex Card' ads to trick seniors into switching out of their traditional Medicare plan without realizing they may lose access to their current doctors.

🏛️ Enterprise & Household Zero-Trust Verification Checklist

Whether defending a household checking account, a retiree retirement portfolio, or a corporate treasury desk, modern social engineering succeeds only when an adversary forces an in-band decision before independent verification occurs. Technical security controls must be paired with strict procedural circuit-breakers that separate the communication channel from the authorization channel.
