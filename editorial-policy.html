⚖️ Editorial Integrity & Research Standards

Editorial Standards, E-E-A-T Methodology & Corrections Policy

ScamShield Lab is an independent cybersecurity research initiative dedicated to democratizing threat intelligence and consumer defense against synthetic fraud. Because cybercrime directly impacts personal safety and financial well-being (YMYL), our editorial guidelines adhere to the highest industry standards of Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness.

🏛️ The Four Pillars of ScamShield Lab E-E-A-T

Every investigation, forensic guide, and interactive heuristic scanner published on ScamShield Lab is governed by our foundational four-pillar framework:

🔍 1. Direct Experience

Our research desk actively analyzes real adversary attack infrastructure—including AiTM reverse-proxy phishing kits (Evilginx, Modlishka), bulletproof hosting clusters, synthetic audio vocoder scripts, and decentralized wallet draining smart contracts. We never publish theoretical assumptions without hands-on forensic telemetry.

🎓 2. Verifiable Expertise

All content is authored and reviewed by credentialed identity architects, computer science academicians, and certified security researchers. Research leadership includes Dr. Priyanka Ameta (Computer Science Academician and AI researcher) and Hemendra K. (Identity and Access Management / Zero-Trust specialist).

📜 3. Authoritative Sourcing

Statistical claims, criminal vector mechanics, and victim loss metrics must be independently verified against primary public enforcement disclosures: FBI IC3 Annual Reports, FTC Consumer Protection Bulletins, CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities, and NIST Special Publications (SP 800-63B).

🛡️ 4. Zero-Trust Transparency

Privacy is mathematically guaranteed through local in-browser client-side JavaScript execution. We never log or store user text inputs on cloud servers. Our source code and heuristic rules are open for public audit on GitHub.

🔬 The Multi-Step Editorial & Peer Review Process

Before any guide, scam alert, or checklist is published on ScamShield Lab, it must successfully navigate a 4-stage verification lifecycle:

  1. Threat Intelligence Triage & Vector Identification: The research desk monitors emerging lures across dark web forums, telecom registries, SMS spam repositories, and academic vulnerability disclosures.
  2. Technical Forensic Analysis: Researchers isolate the underlying psychological mechanism (coercive urgency, authority simulation, artificial scarcity) and technological exploit (lookalike Punycode domains, disposable VoIP ranges, synthetic neural acoustics).
  3. Empathetic Tone & Anti-Victim-Blaming Audit: Guides are audited to ensure victims of deception are never shamed or judged. Action plans focus purely on adversary mechanics and constructive remediation steps.
  4. Peer Review & Citation Verification: A secondary certified practitioner verifies all footnotes, regulatory statutory references (e.g., Regulation E, UCC Article 4A), and ensures recommended countermeasures are technically viable.

💰 Commercial Independence & Affiliate Disclosure

To ensure our AI Forensic Text Analyzer, Emergency Incident Playbook, and Educational Guides remain 100% free with zero paywalls, ScamShield Lab sustains operations through Google AdSense advertising and verified cybersecurity affiliate partnerships.

Our Commercial Independence Guarantee:
  • Commercial sponsors or affiliate programs have zero editorial control over our threat rankings, heuristic scores, or fraud warnings.
  • We never accept paid reviews or sponsored placements for unverified software. Every recommended countermeasure (such as NordVPN Threat Protection, Google Workspace, NordPass, or hardware security keys) is selected based on independent security certifications (AV-Comparatives, SE Labs, FIDO2 certification).
  • All affiliate links are clearly and unambiguously tagged with rel="noopener sponsored" in compliance with Federal Trade Commission (FTC) guidelines.

🔄 Corrections, Updates & Errata Policy

In the rapidly changing threat landscape, accuracy is paramount. We actively welcome feedback, peer review challenges, and corrections from the cybersecurity community.

If you identify a factual error, an outdated government hotline, or a broken source link in any of our published materials:

  • Please email our editorial desk directly at editorial@scamshieldlab.com with the URL and supporting citation.
  • Resolution SLA: Our research team reviews correction submissions within 24 business hours.
  • Transparency: If a material correction is made, a timestamped "Correction Notice" or "Updated" notation is appended to the article header explaining what was modified.
