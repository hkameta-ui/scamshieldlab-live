Knowledge Center

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, authoritative answers to the most common questions regarding AI scams and modern cyber safety.

Can scammers really clone a family member's voice with just a short audio clip? +

Yes. Modern neural voice synthesis models (such as VALL-E, ElevenLabs, and open-source equivalents) require as little as 3 seconds of clear reference audio to replicate pitch, cadence, and emotional inflection. Attackers obtain this audio from public social media videos, voicemail greetings, or brief scam pretext calls.

What is the best way to prove a caller is a real family member? +

The only foolproof defense is an offline Family Safe Word or pre-shared secret code phrase that is never spoken in front of smart speakers or posted online. Alternatively, immediately hang up and dial that person back on their verified saved cell number.

If I sent money via Bitcoin ATM or wire transfer to a scammer, can I get it back? +

Cryptocurrency transactions on blockchains are mathematically immutable and cannot be reversed by any authority. Beware of "Crypto Recovery Services" on Instagram or Telegram; virtually 100% of these services are secondary 'recovery scams' that steal additional fees. For wire transfers, if reported within 24 hours, bank fraud desks can occasionally freeze funds before final foreign clearing.

Does freezing my credit affect my credit score? +

No. Freezing your credit files has zero impact on your credit score, does not prevent you from using existing credit cards, and is completely free under federal law. You can temporarily lift ("thaw") the freeze in minutes whenever you legitimately apply for a mortgage, loan, or credit card.
