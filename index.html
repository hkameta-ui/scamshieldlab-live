Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, authoritative answers to the most common questions regarding AI scams and modern cyber safety.

Can scammers really clone a family member's voice with just a short audio clip? +

Yes. Landmark research by Microsoft Research (VALL-E: Neural Codec Language Models as Zero-Shot Text to Speech Synthesizers, 2023) demonstrated that as few as 3 seconds of reference audio is sufficient to replicate acoustic pitch, cadence, and emotional inflection. Threat projections by the Deloitte Center for Financial Services estimate generative AI fraud and deepfake losses exceed $1.1B+ annually, reinforced by formal consumer alerts from the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and FBI IC3 documenting threat actors harvesting voice audio from public social media videos (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube), voicemail greetings, or brief pretext robocalls.

Official Citations: Deloitte Center for Financial Services ($1.1B+ Deepfake Losses) • Microsoft Research VALL-E (arXiv:2301.02111) • FTC AI Voice Cloning Advisory • FBI IC3 Annual Cyber Threat Report
What is the best way to prove a caller is a real family member? +

The most authoritative, high-assurance countermeasure is an offline Family Safe Word or pre-shared secret code phrase that is never spoken in front of smart speakers or posted online. Alternatively, immediately hang up and dial that person back on their verified saved cell number.

If I sent money via Bitcoin ATM or wire transfer to a scammer, can I get it back? +

Cryptocurrency transactions on blockchains are mathematically immutable and cannot be reversed by any authority. Beware of "Crypto Recovery Services" on Instagram or Telegram; fraudulent secondary recovery syndicates exploit desperate victims to steal additional fees. For wire transfers, if reported within 24 hours, bank fraud desks can occasionally freeze funds before final foreign clearing.

Does freezing my credit affect my credit score? +

No. Freezing your credit files has zero impact on your credit score, does not prevent you from using existing credit cards, and is completely free under federal law. You can temporarily lift ("thaw") the freeze in minutes whenever you legitimately apply for a mortgage, loan, or credit card.
